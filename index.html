Chapter 02 · Continued

Precision medicine for cancer, modelled

Antibody-drug conjugates pair a tumour-seeking antibody with a potent cytotoxic payload, so the drug is delivered to the cancer cell rather than the whole body. Predicting how they behave in a population is a modelling problem as much as a biological one.

I worked with a world-leading scientist on the simulation and reporting for ADC modelling in Simcyp. I built all of the ADC reporting and some of the input screens, and helped implement parts of the core ODE processing, so scientists could model these therapies and see the results.

  • ReportingAll of the ADC reporting, so scientists could interrogate and compare their simulations.
  • Input screensWPF screens for entering ADC parameters.
  • Core engineHelped implement parts of the ODE processing in C++.
Chapter 02 · Continued

Dosing medicines where trials are hard

The Gates Foundation funds research that uses the Simcyp Simulator to work out safe doses for people who are hard to enrol in clinical trials, such as pregnant and breastfeeding women and children in low-income countries. A virtual population answers the question without putting vulnerable patients through a trial.

  • Global healthAn open PBPK model repository, built with the Medicines for Malaria Venture, for dosing in malaria, Zika and sleeping sickness.

The platform I spent thirteen years on, in use on funded global health research.

Chapter 02 · Continued

Ten years of drawing the curves

A large part of my years on Simcyp was output and reporting. Scientists judge a simulation by its plots, so I spent a good ten years turning engine results into charts: concentration against time for a virtual population, with the variability band and observed clinical data overlaid.

They were generated from the C++ engine through COM automation into Excel workbooks, so a scientist could open a result and compare it straight away.

  • CurvesConcentration–time profiles, linear for oral doses and logarithmic for intravenous ones.
  • VariabilityThe simulated population as a band around the mean, not a single line.
  • EvidenceObserved clinical data points overlaid, so agreement is visible at a glance.

A visual editor for marketing

The sales team and lead designer wanted to build branded collages without a developer. I designed the data schema and built a drag-and-drop WYSIWYG editor with a custom mesh-warp engine, in React, TypeScript and Fabric.js on a .NET back end.

It works as an editor for people and as an API for the email render pipeline, so the same layouts that marketing design are what customers receive.

Layers, snapping, rulers and an opacity preview make it feel like a design tool, running in the browser. Built with TypeScript and C#, with Claude Code alongside.
